# SYNTHETIC DEMONSTRATION

## NOT A BENCHMARK RESULT

## NOT SCIENTIFIC VALIDATION

This fictional example illustrates an inspectable reasoning record. It makes no claim about a real product, person, organization, dataset, or research finding.

## Scenario

| Field | Synthetic record |
| --- | --- |
| Claim | “Offering a fictional 14-day trial will increase conversion for the fictional product Atlas Notes.” |
| Initial belief | Plausible, but uncertain. |
| Evidence | A small fictional pilot notes 18 trial starts from one newsletter. No randomized comparison was conducted. |
| Assumption | Newsletter recipients resemble the broader intended audience. |
| Objection | Trial starts do not establish paid conversion, and the audience may already be unusually interested. |
| New / contradictory information | A fictional follow-up finds that most trial users did not convert after the trial period. |
| Evaluation status | **UNDERTESTED** — this is a proposed reviewer annotation for the demonstration, not an automated XOD research classification. |
| Uncertainty | High: the pilot is small, lacks a control, and does not measure the decision outcome directly. |
| Human review | A human should decide whether to run a controlled comparison, revise the claim, or defer the decision. |

## Illustrative belief revision

The initial wording overgeneralizes from trial starts to paid conversion. The new information does not prove that trials never help; it weakens the original claim and changes the next useful question to: “Under what conditions, if any, does the fictional trial improve paid conversion compared with a control?”

## Intended lesson

XOD's current architecture can record claims, evidence, assumptions, objections, new information, uncertainty, and a human-authored belief revision. It does not automatically certify evidence, decide the status of research claims, or replace a reviewer.
